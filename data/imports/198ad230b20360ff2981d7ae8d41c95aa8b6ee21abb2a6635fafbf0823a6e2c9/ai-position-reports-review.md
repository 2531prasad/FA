# Review of the two asymmetric AI investment reports

Prepared 12 October 2026, India time. This review evaluates the reports at their stated 11 October information cutoff and 9 October prices. It does not replace their scenarios with new price targets.

**Overall judgment:** the capitalized-filename report is the better framework for further underwriting. The lowercase-filename report contains material analysis that should be retained, especially Nvidia cash conversion, Marvell, and the optical suppliers. Neither establishes a probability-supported asymmetric investment or a defensible expectation of 55% annual returns.

Report A: [asymmetric-ai-positions-post-october-2026.md](../asymmetric-ai-positions-post-october-2026.md). Report B: [Asymmetric_AI_Positions_Post_October_2026.md](../Asymmetric_AI_Positions_Post_October_2026.md).

**How the conclusions differ**

| Question | Report A | Report B | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leading candidates | NVDA, then AVGO | NVDA, then AVGO | Agreement is conditional on earnings durability and financing quality. |
| Next opportunity | Connectivity/custom silicon: CRDO and MRVL | Memory: MU, followed by TSM and VRT | A substantive disagreement that neither report resolves with a common model. |
| Memory | Normalize the cycle before ranking highly | Promotes MU to third research priority | B makes a stronger demand case, but does not demonstrate durable normalized margins. |
| Networking | ANET is attractive operationally but expensive | Adds the October rack-scale/open-Ethernet announcement | B has the more complete architecture discussion. |
| NAND and storage | Covers HDDs; identifies other storage layers without valuing a NAND specialist | Adds SNDK and explicitly separates memory/storage tiers | B closes a useful coverage gap. |
| Optics | Values COHR and LITE and compares their burdens | Discusses them without a valuation screen | B loses decision-relevant work. |
| Marvell | Detailed thesis, management-target discussion and return calculation | Omitted | Omission is not an explained rejection. |
| Construction | Mostly mechanism watchlist | Adds FIX with earnings and backlog | A useful extension, although backlog and current margins still need underwriting. |
| Concentration | Warns about common capex exposure | Adds loss-budget arithmetic and rotation rules | B is more usable for portfolio decisions; correlated losses remain uncertain. |
| Financing | AVGO backstop and specific NVDA cash-conversion warning | AVGO backstop and general demand-quality monitoring | Combine them; both miss an adjacent AVGO financing disclosure. |

**What both reports get right**

Their strongest contribution is the causal chain from useful AI demand to equipment purchases, supplier profit and eventual per-share cash. That is a better starting point than ranking suppliers by market capitalization or exposure to a growing theme.

Both correctly distinguish efficiency per task from aggregate demand, economic equipment life from accounting depreciation, and electricity consumption from peak power and rack density. Both recognize that more bytes need not mean more storage revenue, and that upstream component revenue is nested inside downstream equipment invoices.

They also recognize that a moving bottleneck can remove an existing supplier's product. Copper, pluggable optics, co-packaged optics, switches and retimers cannot be treated as interchangeable claims on one profit pool. These distinctions should survive any consolidation of the reports.

**The arithmetic is sound; the assumptions drive the disagreement**

I recalculated all 63 three-year scenario cells across the two reports, the 22 reverse-underwriting growth hurdles, and all 14 stated entry-price outputs. The results agree within their displayed rounding. The compounding factors also check: 55% annually produces 3.723875 times capital in three years and 8.946610 times capital in five years.

That verification establishes internal arithmetic consistency. It does not validate the earnings paths or exit multiples.

| Example | Report A assumption | Report B assumption | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| NVDA central case | 30% EPS growth; 25× exit P/E | Same 30% EPS growth; 30× exit P/E | CAGR rises from 28.6% to 36.7% entirely because of the exit multiple. |
| NVDA growth needed for 55% return | 30× exit P/E | 35× exit P/E | Required EPS CAGR falls from 47.4% to 40.1%, with the same starting price and EPS. |
| AVGO central case | 30% EPS growth; 25× exit P/E | 35% EPS growth; 30× exit P/E | Return rises from roughly 26% to 39% through two more favorable assumptions. |
| CRDO earnings screen | Adjusted quarterly EPS annualized | GAAP quarterly EPS annualized | Starting multiple changes from 45.1× to 80.8× without a change in price. |
| MU earnings screen | Latest adjusted quarter annualized | Actual full-year GAAP EPS | Multiple changes from 7.7× to 13.8×, mostly because the earnings period changes. |

Report B is more systematic, but its NVDA and AVGO scenarios are more optimistic. Its labels and cautions do not make its terminal valuations conservative.

Its entry levels also answer a different question from A's. A's NVDA ceiling of approximately $241 assumes its strong-success case; B's approximately $157 entry assumes its central case. Those numbers are compatible conditional calculations. Averaging them would have no analytical meaning.

**A material financing omission in both reports**

Both describe Broadcom's approximately $29 billion maximum potential undiscounted lease-backstop liability. Immediately after that disclosure, the same filing describes a conditional mechanism under which the customer may issue Broadcom convertible promissory notes totaling up to $42 billion for lease-related obligations. None had been issued as of the filing. [Broadcom Q3 Form 10-Q, Note 10](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1730168/000173016826000080/avgo-20260802.htm).

This does not establish a $42 billion loss or justify adding $29 billion and $42 billion into a single exposure. It does mean that underwriting only the backstop is incomplete. The transaction map needs to identify overlapping obligations, funding conditions, recourse, collateral, maturity, conversion terms and the treatment of customer default.

Both reports are right to make AVGO conditional on credit review. Neither completes that review.

**NVDA: preserve A's cash warning**

A correctly identifies Q2 operating cash flow of $24.1 billion against $59.7 billion GAAP net income, with $22.3 billion absorbed by receivables. This deserves more prominence than B's general monitoring language. The release also confirms that Nvidia's adjusted earnings now retain stock compensation. [Nvidia Q2 FY2027 results](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-second-quarter-fiscal-2027).

Weak conversion in one quarter does not establish bad revenue. It creates a question about collection timing, customer credit and product-transition working capital. The next model should reconcile receivable aging, subsequent collections and cash conversion over several quarters before equating the displayed earnings multiple with a cash-flow bargain.

**MU: a genuine disagreement, not a numerical error**

The current disclosed earnings are extraordinary: Q4 GAAP gross margin was 86.8%; full-year GAAP EPS was $74.33; Q4 GAAP EPS was $32.87. [Micron FY2026 results](https://investors.micron.com/news/press-release/2026/Micron-Technology-Inc--Reports-Record-Fiscal-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2026-Results/).

At the reports' $1,029 price, full-year EPS gives 13.8× earnings, while annualizing the latest GAAP quarter gives 7.8×. These are different denominators, neither a normalized earnings estimate.

B's central terminal EPS of $100 is about 24% below the latest GAAP quarter annualized, yet above the completed fiscal year's earnings. It already embeds some normalization relative to the recent peak. It does not show why $100 is sustainable, or which combination of volumes, prices, product mix and factory investment produces it.

Promoting MU into serious research is justified by the mechanisms discussed. Ranking it third on expected investment attractiveness remains unproved. The necessary comparison is normalized shareholder cash across memory demand/supply regimes, not MU's displayed P/E against NVDA's.

**CRDO: turn the accounting caveat into a model**

The GAAP/adjusted EPS difference is real: $0.67 versus $1.20. The release also discloses $88.0 million of share-based compensation, approximately 18.4% of revenue. [Credo Q1 FY2027 results and reconciliation](https://investors.credosemi.com/news-events/news/news-details/2026/Credo-Technology-Group-Holding-Ltd-Reports-First-Quarter-of-Fiscal-Year-2027-Financial-Results/default.aspx).

A's adjusted valuation makes CRDO look materially less demanding than B's GAAP valuation. Neither basis alone supplies comparable owner earnings: acquired-intangible amortization, integration costs and recurring compensation need separate treatment. Model the compensation economics and future shares consistently; do not ignore compensation or count the same cost twice.

Customer concentration and components retained in future designs matter at least as much as current revenue growth. A connectivity category thesis is insufficient to establish CRDO's particular profit capture.

**ANET: B adds relevant evidence, not proof of monetization**

B's October development is verified. Arista announced rack-scale Ethernet for scale-up and scale-out, support for up to 144 accelerators per rack, and a choice between EOS and open network operating systems. [Arista's 7 October announcement](https://www.arista.com/en/company/news/press-release/24818-pr-20261007).

This expands the plausible opportunity beyond conventional scale-out networking. It also raises a question about which software and systems value Arista retains when customers choose an open operating system. The announcement does not resolve pricing, revenue timing or margin capture. Both reports' price sensitivity remains reasonable on their stated screens.

**SNDK: B improves coverage, but cash quality needs specificity**

B's NAND addition is useful. Sandisk attributes approximately two-thirds of its sequential revenue increase to pricing. Its reconciliation also removes $1.938 billion of quarterly new-business-model prepayments/deposits in calculating adjusted free cash flow. [Sandisk Q4 FY2026 results](https://www.sandisk.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/2026/2026-08-05-sandisk-reports-fiscal-fourth-quarter-2026-financial-results).

Those facts make contract terms and advance-payment economics central to the thesis. Prepayments can improve funding and indicate commitment; they do not automatically demonstrate permanent pricing power. B's general warning about advances should become a quantified bridge from reported cash to recurring shareholder cash.

**The shared analytical gaps**

1. **Asymmetry remains unestablished.** The reports explicitly provide sensitivities rather than probability-weighted forecasts. That is transparent, but a large bull payoff alone cannot establish favorable asymmetry. Neither quantifies the likelihood of the required outcome or its relationship to permanent impairment.
2. **Earnings definitions and periods are inconsistent.** A uses issuer-adjusted quarterly run rates broadly. B mixes GAAP and adjusted figures, completed fiscal years, quarterly run rates, guidance and annualized half-year earnings. Each is labeled, but labeling does not make the ranking comparable.
3. **Terminal P/Es lack sufficient company-specific justification.** Growth, competitive durability, interest rates, reinvestment requirements and cyclicality should inform the exit range. A high multiple applied to peak cyclical EPS can compound two optimistic assumptions.
4. **EPS is not built from operations.** The reports mostly assume EPS growth rather than derive revenue, market share, margins, tax, financing, reinvestment and diluted shares. B's absolute-scale check is useful but should be applied to every central and bull case.
5. **The ranking objective is unclear.** Research priority, strongest business, highest potential payoff and best expected return are different. For example, B gives AVGO a higher central modeled return than NVDA while ranking NVDA first. That can be rational, but the risk adjustment is qualitative.
6. **The market's expectation is asserted rather than measured.** Low run-rate P/E is not evidence that the market misunderstands the business. The missing question is which durable earnings path the current price implies and why that path is too pessimistic.
7. **Three-year price models do not fully cover a three-to-five-year mandate.** A longer horizon should have its own deployment and reinvestment path, rather than simply extending a short high-growth assumption.

The reports' 55% hurdle also needs interpretation. A reported historical account XIRR does not establish the expected opportunity available to new capital. If 55% is a hard minimum for the central underwriting case, none of B's displayed central cases qualifies at its quoted entry prices. That is a result of the model, not a reason to force more optimistic inputs.

**The scale hurdle is substantial**

B's NVDA reverse-underwriting example requires approximately $24.4 annual EPS at a 35× terminal multiple. At its assumed roughly 24.3 billion shares and a hypothetical 50% net margin, that entails about $1.19 trillion of annual revenue. This is conditional arithmetic from the report, not a forecast. At a 30× exit multiple, the same return hurdle would require about $1.38 trillion under the same share-count and margin assumptions.

Relative cheapness does not remove this absolute economic burden. A serious model must explain who purchases that output and how customers earn enough to sustain purchases.

**How I would use the reports**

| Candidate group | What the combined evidence supports | Work needed before treating it as a leading investment |
|---|---|---|
| NVDA | Most coherent first research candidate in these reports | Cash collection, independent customer demand and sustainable system-level margins. |
| AVGO | Strong alternative architecture thesis with material credit conditions | Full financing map, including notes; semiconductor economics separated from software. |
| MU / SNDK | Credible moving-bottleneck and pricing-cycle research | Product-level supply, enforceable contracts, normalized pricing and cash after reinvestment. |
| CRDO / MRVL / COHR | Conditional connectivity/custom-silicon/optics opportunities | Comparable economic earnings, customer/program concentration and future product content. |
| ANET / TSM / VRT | Strong mechanisms with distinct valuation and failure modes | Evidence that supports the price and return hurdle, rather than category relevance alone. |
| AMD / ALAB / HDD suppliers / FIX | Separate theses requiring their own proof | Entry burden, retained profit, dilution and cycle sensitivity. |

This is an ordering of research questions, not an allocation or a new set of buy recommendations.

Use B's organization and scenario transparency as the base. Restore A's cash-conversion discussion, Marvell analysis, optics valuation and financing context. Rebuild a common economic-earnings and cash-flow screen before deciding whether memory or connectivity deserves the next concentrated position.
